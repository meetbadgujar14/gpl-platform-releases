Key Findings
01
A quality breach requiring immediate attention is present in this period's inbound activity.
Of 28 total goods receipts processed, 2 were fully rejected and 4 were partially accepted, meaning 21% of receipts did not clear cleanly. At the unit level, 1,520 units out of 34,535 received were rejected, compounding a supplier defect rate of 3% and placing USD 2,507 in standard cost exposure on rejected inventory alone. The on-time delivery rate of 91% adds further pressure, meaning nearly 1 in 10 shipments arrived late, and 2 purchase order lines remain partially received against 11,865 units ordered with only 11,155 confirmed received, leaving a 710-unit shortfall outstanding.
02
This performance is below acceptable thresholds.
The primary driver is supplier quality, with a 3% defect rate generating 132 damaged units across shipment lines and forcing the partial or full rejection of 6 receipts. While the 97% acceptance rate appears adequate, the absolute volume of 1,520 rejected units against available inventory of 34,028 out of 41,395 on hand signals that nearly 7,367 units are currently unavailable, likely tied to quarantine or quality holds.
03
The business risk is stock availability tightening if rejected and held inventory is not resolved quickly.
The required action is to formally dispute the 2 rejected receipts with the responsible suppliers this week and escalate the 710-unit PO shortfall for expedited fulfillment.
