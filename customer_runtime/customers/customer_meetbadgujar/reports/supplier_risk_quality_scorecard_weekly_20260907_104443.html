Key Findings
01
The most urgent issue flagged in this scorecard is the supplier defect rate, which stands at 81 percent.
This is a critical RED alert and demands immediate attention. A defect rate this high is far outside any acceptable range for a healthy supply chain, and it is directly reflected in the goods receipt data, where 1,520 units were rejected across 2 rejected and 4 partially accepted receipts, against 33,015 units accepted. The primary driver appears to be concentrated quality failures among a subset of suppliers, given that only 9 of the 13 active suppliers have performance data recorded, meaning visibility across the supply base is incomplete.
02
The lead time picture is more mixed.
The average lead time of 7 days is reasonable, but the maximum lead time stretches to 38 days against a minimum of 5 days, tracked across 2,246 orders. This spread suggests some suppliers are significantly slower than others, creating delivery unpredictability. Total supplier spend sits at USD77.6 lakh, with total orders valued at USD11,751 and procurement spend at USD2.9 lakh. The 2 cancelled purchase orders indicate some disruption.
03
The business cannot absorb an 81 percent defect rate without serious cost and customer service consequences.
The immediate action required is to identify which suppliers are responsible for the 1,520 rejected units and place them on a formal corrective action plan or consider suspension.
