Key Findings
01
There are 6 goods receipt quality concerns requiring immediate attention.
Across 9 suppliers tracked, 2 receipts were fully rejected and 4 were partially accepted, resulting in 1,520 units rejected against 33,015 units accepted. This rejection volume is unusual relative to the otherwise strong 97% quality acceptance rate and 3% defect rate, suggesting the rejections are concentrated rather than spread evenly across the supplier base.
02
The primary cause appears to be a small number of underperforming deliveries pulling down an otherwise healthy picture.
Lead times vary significantly, ranging from 5 days at best to 38 days at worst against an average of 7 days, which points to inconsistency from specific suppliers rather than a systemic problem. On-time delivery sits at 91% across 2,246 tracked orders, which is acceptable but leaves room for improvement.
03
The business impact is manageable but not trivial.
Total supplier spend stands at USD 77.6 lakh with USD 11,751 in tracked order value and USD 2.9 lakh in procurement spend. With 2 cancelled purchase orders and 1,520 rejected units, there is a real cost in rework, delays, and reordering that erodes margin.
04
Only 9 of 13 active suppliers have performance data on record.
The immediate action required is to identify which suppliers are responsible for the 1,520 rejected units and the 38-day lead time outlier, and place them on a formal performance review before the next order cycle.
